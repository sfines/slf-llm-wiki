# Long-Term Strategic Economic Planning

Long-Term Strategic Economic Planning in agent simulations refers to the capability of an artificial agent to look beyond immediate optimization (such as current-period consumption) and formulate multi-step abstracts and goals for future stability.

## Overcoming Short-Termism
Traditional LLM-driven agents often treat each simulation step (e.g., a simulated month) as an independent, rational choice puzzle. This leads to agents that react wildly to transient market disturbances, causing simulation instability and large-amplitude oscillations in aggregate metrics. 

## The Role of Strategic Planning
Robust economic systems are fundamentally based on long-term decision-making. By integrating memory retention, belief revision, and continuous persona updates, agents can plan strategically. They absorb multi-year shocks and regime shifts (such as a simulated pandemic) by altering their long-term behavior—like prioritizing savings over consumption—rather than over-correcting at every individual time step. This stability allows the overall simulation to more accurately reflect real-world, long-horizon human planning.
